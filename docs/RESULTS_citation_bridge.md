# Results: Citation Bridge Between Polycrisis and Limits to Growth

Generated by `scripts/06_citation_bridge.py`. Every number below was computed by the script. The reference lists were refetched from OpenAlex on October 24, 2026.

## Question

Which papers draw on both intellectual traditions? The test is whether a paper's reference list contains works from each tradition's own base of most cited sources.

## Definitions

* **Corpora.** Polycrisis: `data/polycrisis_papers.csv`. Limits to Growth (LtG): `data/ltg_refined_papers.csv` (the refined pull, not the noisy `ltg_papers.csv`). Papers are deduplicated by OpenAlex id across corpora.
* **Reference list.** The union of the refetched list and the list saved in the original raw file. Both are OpenAlex records of the same work's references, and the two differ for some papers (see Reference coverage).
* **Record merge (primary).** OpenAlex often holds several records of the same work, so records are merged before anything is counted. (a) Corpus papers are merged by normalised title plus first author surname; a paper with no first author on record is never merged, and two corpus papers with different first authors are never merged. (b) Cited works in the pool, meaning works cited by at least 3 papers within one corpus, polycrisis or LtG (969 works; a further 217 works cited 3 or more times across both corpora together but under 3 within each are not checked), are merged by normalised title, plus one further rule for a title equal to the part before the colon of longer titles. (c) A merged group counts as one work when it is cited and as one paper when it is tested, with the union of its members' reference lists. A merged cited work is shown with the earliest year and the type of its earliest record, and a merged corpus paper takes the year, cited_by_count, type, title and id of its most cited member record. The exact rules are `build_gmap` in `scripts/06_citation_bridge.py`. Results are shown with merged records (primary) and with unmerged records.
* **Canon (primary).** For each corpus, count how many of its papers cite each work. Canon_P is the top 50 works cited by polycrisis papers. Canon_L is the top 50 works cited by LtG papers. Ties at the cutoff are broken by OpenAlex id. No works were picked by hand.
* **Shared canon.** Works that appear in both top N lists. They are removed from both canons for the bridge test, so a bridge paper must cite something distinctive to each tradition, not only the works both fields share.
* **Sensitivity.** The same test at N = 25 and N = 100. Variant 2 builds each canon from the N most cited papers inside each corpus by `cited_by_count` (OpenAlex citations from anywhere), again removing papers that land in both lists.
* **Bridge paper.** A corpus paper with at least one reference in Canon_P and at least one in Canon_L. A stricter sensitivity version needs at least 2 references in Canon_P and at least 2 in Canon_L (column Min refs to each canon = 2 below), with the same denominator.
* **Denominator.** Papers with a nonempty reference list, never all papers.

### Canon work years and the trend

The canon is made from what the corpus papers cite, and 84.8% of polycrisis papers with a year are from 2024 or later, so the canon can hold recent works that older papers could not cite. Canon_P works (after shared removal) were published from 1973 to 2024 (median 2018.0, year known for 42 of 43 works); Canon_L works from 1931 to 2020 (median 2006.0, year known for 40 of 43 works). Pooled, the median canon work year is 2012.0 and the newest is 2024. A paper published before a canon work cannot cite it, so low shares in early periods are partly mechanical.

Counting only papers published in 2018 or later (the higher of the two canon medians), 51 of 854 (6.0%). Counting only papers published in 2024 or later (the newest canon work year), 33 of 608 (5.4%). The period shares are 2000 to 2014 0.7%, 2015 to 2019 3.9%, 2020 to 2022 4.2%, 2023 to 2024 7.5%, 2025 to 2026 5.2%. ## Limitations

* **Reference coverage.** Only 1122 of 2458 papers after merging (45.6%) have any reference list in OpenAlex. The bridge share is computed only over those. Papers without a list cannot be tested here, and nothing is claimed about them.
* **OpenAlex reference completeness.** The saved and refetched reference lists are different sets for 472 papers and one is a strict subset of the other for 118 more. Coverage differs by document type. By type (papers with a reference list out of all papers; the 6 most common of 16 types): `article` 830 of 1573; `book-chapter` 185 of 442; `preprint` 42 of 122; `book` 9 of 102; `other` 4 of 68; `peer-review` 11 of 32.
* **Books and chapters.** Types of the 93 canon works in the two top lists before shared removal: `article` 68; `book` 16; `unresolved` 4; `book-review` 2; `preprint` 2; `book-chapter` 1. A book cited by a paper counts like any other work. Books themselves rarely have reference lists: `book` 9 of 102 papers have one (8.8%), and books are outside the denominator for the most part. Chapters are more often covered: `book-chapter` 185 of 442 (41.9%). Even so, 257 chapters have no list and cannot be tested.
* **Corpus query scope.** The corpora come from keyword searches, so the polycrisis corpus holds papers that use that word and the LtG corpus holds papers that matched the refined query. Canon_P and Canon_L describe what these two retrieved sets cite, not every paper in either field. The LtG refined filter in `scripts/01b_fetch_ltg_refined.py` keeps any paper whose text contains a strong marker, including polycrisis and degrowth. In the LtG corpus the median publication year is 2013.0, 84.0% of papers with a year are from 2022 or earlier, and 35.3% are from before 2010. In the polycrisis corpus the median year is 2025.0 and 84.8% of papers with a year are from 2024 or later.
* **Circularity.** The canon is derived from the same corpus it is then tested on. A bridge here means citing the corpus's own frequent works from each side, not citing an outside list of founding texts. 1 of the 53 bridge papers is itself a canon work.
* **Low bridge bar.** The main definition needs only one reference on each side. Requiring at least 2 on each side gives 8 bridge papers at N = 50 instead of 53 with merged records (10 instead of 55 unmerged).
* **Baseline.** The baselines are the two random reference draws above, which ignore topic within each field. There is no comparison with a different pair of fields, so the bridge counts say how often both canons are cited, not whether that is more than usual for two related fields.
* **Unresolved titles.** 61 of the 969 works checked for duplicate records have no resolved title, so they cannot be merged.
* **Duplicate records.** Merging uses titles (and first author for papers) and was only run on works cited by at least 3 papers within one corpus, so duplicates of less cited works are not merged, and different works with identical titles could be merged by mistake. The merged groups among the canon are listed above.
* **Canon definition.** A canon built from counts depends on corpus size and composition. A paper in both corpora counts toward both counts. Sensitivity runs above show how much the answer moves with N and with the variant.
* **Refetch gaps.** 06a asked OpenAlex for every id; 131 of 2616 ids returned no record, and for those the saved list was used. Titles could not be resolved for 4 of 93 canon works.
* **Thin canons.** At N = 50 the cutoff is 7 citing papers for Canon_P and 8 for Canon_L, and several works tie at the cutoff, so which tied works are in the canon is decided by id order. At N = 50 bridge counts are 53 (id order), 46 (strict canon) and 54.3 on average with random ties (5th to 95th 53 to 57); see the cutoff ties section. Treat the canon as a rough core, not a settled list.
* **Partial 2026.** The latest publication date in the data is March 29, 2026, so 2026 holds only part of a year.
