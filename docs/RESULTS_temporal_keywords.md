# Results: Temporal Keyword Convergence

Generated by `scripts/07_temporal_keywords.py`. Every number below was computed by the script.

## Question

Is the vocabulary of the polycrisis literature and the Limits to Growth (LtG) literature converging over time?

## Method

* **Keyword source.** The same as the 21 of 50 headline in `scripts/05_simple_viz.py`, except that repeated OpenAlex ids are kept once (counts differ from 05's saved counts for 12 polycrisis and 22 LtG terms in the saved top 50, see the consistency check): the `keywords` and `concepts` columns of `data/polycrisis_papers.csv` and `data/ltg_refined_papers.csv`, lowercased, longer than 2 characters, counted once per paper, then the stopword list and the drop of any term containing a bracket from 05. These are the OpenAlex keyword and concept tags saved in the CSVs, not author keywords.
* **Consistency check.** For the full period, the top 50 lists of the two corpora share 21 terms with alphabetical tie breaking, recomputed from the paper CSVs. Using the saved frequency files exactly as 05 does gives 21. These share the count but not the terms: the top 50 sets differ by 2 polycrisis and 2 LtG terms per side. Tie sensitivity of this headline: counting only terms above the cutoff count gives 20; random tie breaking over 1000 draws gives a mean of 20.8 (5th to 95th percentile 20 to 22). Full period ties: polycrisis cutoff count 19 shared by 5 terms for 2 open slots; LtG cutoff count 17 shared by 6 terms for 5 open slots. Why this differs from 05's saved counts (checked in code): counting every CSV row reproduces 05's saved counts with 0 polycrisis and 0 LtG mismatches in the 50 terms of each saved top list. Keeping each OpenAlex id once, as this script does, changes 12 polycrisis and 22 LtG term counts in the 50 terms of each saved top list. The cause is the repeated ids in the CSVs (1500 polycrisis rows for 1488 unique ids, 1154 LtG rows for 1135), which 05's route counts more than once and this script counts once. As a result this script's top 50 lists have 2 polycrisis and 2 LtG terms that 05's lists lack, and the reverse; ties at the cutoff add to this. 02 also stores empty keyword cells as the text nan, which 05 drops by accident and this script treats as empty.
* **Periods.** Chosen after seeing the year counts, so every period has about 50 or more papers in both corpora (the script stops if a period has fewer than 45). Papers without a year (15 polycrisis, 25 LtG) are not placed in any period.
* **Measures per period.** Top 50 overlap is reported three ways: with alphabetical tie breaking (this script's tie rule; 05 breaks ties by first seen order, so the full period count of 21 matches 05's 21 only as a count, and the top 50 sets differ by 2 polycrisis and 2 LtG terms per side; shown for comparison only), with the strict set (only terms whose count is above the cutoff count), and with random tie breaking (1000 seeded draws, mean and 5th to 95th percentile). Readings use the random tie breaking numbers. Weighted Jaccard uses all keywords by share of papers (sum of the smaller share divided by sum of the larger share), and cosine similarity uses keyword vectors in which each paper adds a total weight of 1 spread over its keywords; neither depends on a top 50 cutoff.
* **Size matched control.** Because the corpora differ in size, the script also draws, without replacement, the smaller of 60 and the tagged papers available from each corpus in each row (300 draws) and measures the top 50 overlap with random tie breaking. 60 is below the smallest main period corpus (75 papers). Seed 20261024.
* **Full period row.** Uses the same rule as the period rows: tagged papers only. That is 1338 polycrisis and 963 LtG papers, including 14 and 24 with no year, which the period rows cannot include.

## Plain reading

In plain terms: the highest tie robust overlap of the two fields' top 50 keywords is in 2023 to 2024 (27.0 shared keywords), and the lowest is in up to 2022 (12.3). The single year rows give 2023 17.2, 2024 19.0, and 2025 to 2026 gives 17.1. The highest single year or last period value is 19.0, against the pooled 27.0. With duplicate records merged by title and first author the period series is 12.9, 27.5, 20.6 instead of 12.3, 27.0, 17.1. The single year and last period values differ by at most 1.9 keywords, and the duplicate merge moves the last period by +3.5, so this note makes no claim of a trend in either direction. 7 papers are in both corpora; each is counted in both corpora's periods and they are not tested separately. Periods differ a lot in paper counts, so read the details below with the size and tie caveats.

Duplicate record sensitivity: this script keeps one row per OpenAlex id, while 06 finds more records of the same work. Applying the same title and first author rule here (06 enforces the same first author check for every corpus paper merge) removes 103 more polycrisis and 54 more LtG records (89 and 47 of them tagged). Tie robust overlap with those merged: up to 2022 12.9 (11 to 15) against 12.3 unmerged; 2023 to 2024 27.5 (26 to 29) against 27.0 unmerged; 2025 to 2026 20.6 (20 to 21) against 17.1 unmerged; full period 22.5 (22 to 24) against 20.8. The main tables use the id only rule. Which record of a merged work is kept: highest cited_by_count, then most distinct terms, then lowest OpenAlex id. Merged groups: 87 polycrisis and 46 LtG, of which 66 and 16 have the top two records tied on cited_by_count, so the second and third parts of the rule decided them.

Top 50 overlap with random tie breaking: up to 2022 12.3 (10 to 14), 2023 to 2024 27.0 (26 to 28), 2025 to 2026 17.1 (15 to 20). Strict set overlap: up to 2022 9, 2023 to 2024 19, 2025 to 2026 10. Cosine: up to 2022 0.386, 2023 to 2024 0.758, 2025 to 2026 0.550. The tie robust overlap, the size matched overlap and the cosine similarity do not all rise at every step with separated ranges, so the data do not show a steady rise. Size matched cosine means: up to 2022 0.343, 2023 to 2024 0.579, 2025 to 2026 0.398; size matched weighted Jaccard means: up to 2022 0.119, 2023 to 2024 0.192, 2025 to 2026 0.145. Tie robust means: up to 2022 12.3, 2023 to 2024 27.0, 2025 to 2026 17.1. Size matched means: up to 2022 10.6, 2023 to 2024 17.1, 2025 to 2026 12.7.

Size matched control: each row draws the same number of papers from each corpus, without replacement (300 draws, random tie breaking): 60 for up to 2022, 60 for 2023 to 2024, 60 for 2025 to 2026; for the single year rows 47 for 2023, 39 for 2024 (the smaller of 60 and the tagged papers available in each corpus). Overlap at that size: up to 2022 10.6 (5th to 95th 8 to 14); 2023 to 2024 17.1 (5th to 95th 13 to 21); 2025 to 2026 12.7 (5th to 95th 9 to 16). Size matched cosine: up to 2022 0.343 (5th to 95th 0.277 to 0.407) against observed 0.386; 2023 to 2024 0.579 (5th to 95th 0.484 to 0.663) against observed 0.758; 2025 to 2026 0.398 (5th to 95th 0.305 to 0.487) against observed 0.550. Size matched weighted Jaccard: up to 2022 0.119 (5th to 95th 0.097 to 0.142) against observed 0.138; 2023 to 2024 0.192 (5th to 95th 0.162 to 0.221) against observed 0.229; 2025 to 2026 0.145 (5th to 95th 0.119 to 0.170) against observed 0.177. Because the size is the same in every period, this series can be compared across periods without the unequal size problem; the observed numbers cannot. It is not a correction of the observed numbers. The gap between observed and size matched overlap mixes two things, using all papers instead of 60 and the unequal sizes of the two corpora, and the two cannot be separated here. The spread is understated where a draw takes a large share of its corpus, because draws then overlap heavily and the range reflects the draw, not full sampling uncertainty. Draws that take at least half of their corpus: 60 of 75 polycrisis papers in up to 2022; 60 of 86 LtG papers in 2023 to 2024; 60 of 82 LtG papers in 2025 to 2026. In the single year rows, the draw takes 47 of 47 LtG papers in 2023 (the entire tagged set, so that side has no sampling variation); the draw takes 39 of 39 LtG papers in 2024 (the entire tagged set, so that side has no sampling variation).

The first period has 75 polycrisis papers and its tie robust overlap range is 10 to 14. The LtG side is small in the later periods: 86 tagged papers in 2023 to 2024 and 82 tagged papers in 2025 to 2026, compared with 771 in up to 2022. The LtG 2023 to 2026 slice is 168 tagged papers, which is 21.8% of the earlier LtG bin. This is the main confound when comparing periods, because the size matched ranges show how far a 60 paper sample moves the overlap. The widest tie robust 5th to 95th range in any period is 5 keywords and the widest size matched range is 8. The duplicate record merge also moves the last period: 20.6 instead of 17.1 (+3.5) in 2025 to 2026, so that period is sensitive to the record rule.

## Limitations

* **Tag counts differ by period.** The overlap is computed from tags taken as given from the OpenAlex records. In the data, the mean number of distinct terms per tagged paper is up to 2022 5.3 polycrisis and 5.2 LtG; 2023 to 2024 4.5 polycrisis and 4.4 LtG; 2025 to 2026 5.1 polycrisis and 5.3 LtG, and the share of papers with no tags is up to 2022 10.7% polycrisis and 16.7% LtG; 2023 to 2024 13.2% polycrisis and 11.3% LtG; 2025 to 2026 8.4% polycrisis and 5.7% LtG. If tagging differs between periods or corpora, a change in overlap may come from tagging as well as from what authors write. This note does not test how the tags were produced.
* **Corpus filter.** The LtG refined corpus was filtered in `scripts/01b_fetch_ltg_refined.py` on text that includes the same kinds of tags counted here (the full keyword and concept lists in 01b; the capped lists, at most 10 keywords and 5 concepts, here), together with the title and abstract: a paper containing a strong marker such as polycrisis is kept, and a paper whose text contains a biology or ecology marker such as ecology is rejected unless a strong marker is present.
* **Broad tags.** Shared top keywords can be broad labels instead of shared ideas. The full period strict shared set is: archaeology; capitalism; climate change; corporate governance; development economics; ecology; economic geography; economic growth; economic system; economics; economy; environmental ethics; environmental planning; environmental resource management; futures contract; natural resource economics; political economy; politics; sustainability; sustainable development. Whether these reflect shared ideas is not tested here.
* **Partial 2026.** The latest publication date in the data is March 29, 2026, so the last period is not a complete two years.
* **Small early bins.** Polycrisis papers: 22 up to 2019 and 84 up to 2022, out of 1488, so the earliest polycrisis period is small and merged. The first period holds 926 LtG papers published 1957 to 2022 (median year 2012.0; 27.0% before 2000), so it mixes many years.
* **Unequal sizes.** The two corpora differ in size within each period (see the papers per period table). The size matched control gives a like for like series across periods but does not correct the observed numbers.
* **Ties.** The most terms left out at the cutoff in any period and corpus is 22 (see the tie table), so the alphabetical top 50 overlap is a single draw from a range. Readings here use the random tie breaking range.
* **Corpus scope.** The corpora are the results of keyword queries, so overlap between them describes these retrieved sets, not the whole of either field. 7 papers are in both corpora.
* **Truncated tag lists.** Checked against the raw API records (2616 papers): 691 have more than 10 OpenAlex keywords (the most is 26), and the fetch script keeps only the first 10 in the CSV, so those papers lose tags. The fetch script also keeps only the 5 highest scoring concepts; 1965 raw records have more than 5 concepts.
* **Untagged papers.** Papers with no keyword or concept tags are left out, and the share of such papers may differ by period and corpus.
* **Duplicate records.** Repeated records of the same work are removed by OpenAlex id only in the main tables. The title and first author rule would remove 103 more polycrisis and 54 more LtG records; the sensitivity above shows the effect.
* **Bin choice.** The period bins were chosen after looking at the year counts, to keep enough papers in both corpora.
