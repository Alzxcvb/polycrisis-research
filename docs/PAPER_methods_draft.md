## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Data source and retrieval

We used OpenAlex, an open index of scholarly works, through its public works endpoint (`https://api.openalex.org/works`). Two corpora were built from two title and abstract searches. The polycrisis corpus comes from the filter `title_and_abstract.search:polycrisis`. The Limits to Growth (LtG) corpus starts from the filter `title_and_abstract.search:"limits to growth"`, in which the phrase is in double quotes in the code, which was then filtered as described in Section 2.2. The filter strings contain no date, language, document type or field restriction.

### 2.2 Corpus construction and filtering

The broad LtG search returns works from biology, ecology and medicine that use the phrase for cell growth, plant growth and similar topics. As a reconstruction from the saved broad pull (not printed by a script), the tiered filter below marks 7,570 of the 12,889 distinct broad ids as biology noise. The LtG corpus is therefore a filtered set built from the broad search. It is not purely a subset of the saved broad pull: 16 of the 1,135 ids in the refined file are not in the saved broad pull. The filter reads the lower cased title, the rebuilt abstract, all OpenAlex keywords and all OpenAlex concepts of each record as one text, and tests marker strings by substring match (not whole word match). The tiered filter has four rules, applied in this order.

1. A record that contains any of 15 strong markers is kept, even if biology terms are present. The strong markers are `meadows`, `donella`, `jorgen randers`, `forrester`, `club of rome`, `world3`, `world 3`, `herrington`, `nebel`, `turner`, `overshoot and collapse`, `finite world`, `polycrisis`, `degrowth` and `post-growth`.
2. Otherwise, a record that contains any of 68 biology, ecology, medicine or chemistry markers is rejected. The list covers ecological and microbial terms, cell and cancer biology, plant and crop terms, animal biology, molecular biology, nutrient limitation and clinical terms.
3. Otherwise, a record that contains any of 11 medium markers is kept: `planetary boundaries`, `system dynamics`, `systems thinking`, `ecological footprint`, `carrying capacity`, `steady state economy`, `ecological economics`, `sustainability transition`, `industrial output`, `nonrenewable resources` and `overshoot`.
4. Otherwise, a record is kept if its title is shorter than 120 characters and contains the exact phrase "limits to growth". All other records are rejected.

The script returns the rule that decided each record and prints a tally of the rules (the tally is not saved with the records). It also has a mode (`--refilter`) that reapplies the rules to the saved refined raw file without the network and then overwrites that raw file and the refined CSV with the kept records, so records it drops cannot be recovered from them.

OpenAlex often holds several records for one work. As a reconstruction from the corpus records (not printed by the script), among the merged groups that contain corpus records, 25 hold both an `article` record and a `preprint` record and 8 hold both an `article` record and a `book-chapter` record, by the type in the corpus CSV. The bridge analysis therefore merges records before it counts anything. The rules are as follows. Corpus papers merge when they have the same normalised title (at least 12 characters) and the same first author surname, and a paper with no first author on record never merges. A normalised title is the title with accents decomposed (NFKD), lower cased, every run of characters other than a to z and 0 to 9 replaced by one space, and spaces trimmed. The first author surname is the last word of the first name in the CSV `authors` column, with any text after an opening bracket removed, normalised in the same way. Cited works that are cited by at least 3 papers within one corpus, counted on unmerged reference lists (969 works, called the pool; a further 217 works are cited 3 or more times across both corpora together but fewer than 3 times within each, and are not checked) merge when they have the same normalised title, with one further rule: a bare title that equals the part before the colon of longer titles joins the group of longer titles that is cited by the most corpus papers. A union is refused if it would join two corpus papers with different first author surnames. A merged group counts as one work when cited and as one paper when tested, with the union of its members' reference lists. A merged corpus paper takes its year, `cited_by_count`, type, title and `paper_id` from its most cited member record (the unit key itself is the representative id described below, and the two differ for 12 of the 134 merged corpus units; this count was computed for this draft by rerunning `build_gmap` and `make_context` on the saved files and comparing `paper_id` with the unit key, and it is not printed by the script), and a merged cited work is shown with the earliest year and the type of its earliest record. The representative id of a group, which is the unit key, is the member cited by the most corpus papers, with ties broken by id text order. A reference from a merged paper to the paper itself is dropped. After merging, the corpus holds 2,458 papers: 1,385 in the polycrisis corpus, 1,080 in the LtG corpus and 7 in both. In total 168 groups were merged, covering 364 ids. The exact rules are in `build_gmap` and `make_context` in `scripts/06_citation_bridge.py`. Results are also computed without merging.

### 2.3 Reference lists

For each paper the analysis uses the union of two reference lists: the list saved in the original raw file and the list from the October 24, 2026 refetch. The union keeps the order of first appearance and removes repeats. When one id occurs more than once in the raw files, the longest saved list is used. In the saved raw files, 809 of 1,500 polycrisis records and 688 of 1,154 LtG records had an empty reference list before the refetch. For comparison the bridge script also computes three other rules, at N = 50 only: the refetched list if it is nonempty and the saved list otherwise (merged and unmerged records, one and two references per side); the saved list only; and the longer of the two lists, with the refetched list used when the lengths are equal (both of these on unmerged records, one and two references per side).

### 2.4 Citation bridge analysis

The aim of this analysis is to find corpus papers whose reference lists contain works from the base of frequently cited sources of each tradition. The base is called a canon, and it is built from the data, with no work chosen by hand.

<!-- src: RESULTS_citation_bridge.md:7, 14; scripts/06_citation_bridge.py:1-17 -->

**Canon.** For each corpus we count, for every cited work, the number of that corpus's papers that cite it (a paper counts once per work, and a paper in both corpora counts in both). Canon_P is the N works cited by the most polycrisis papers. Canon_L is the N works cited by the most LtG papers. Counting is done on merged records and, as a comparison, on unmerged records. N = 50 is the value used for the detailed tables and for the trend and year checks. N = 25 and N = 100 are run as sensitivity, and the tie analysis is run at all three values. Ties at the cutoff count are broken by the OpenAlex id in text (string) order, not numeric order. A second canon definition (Variant 2) takes the N most cited papers inside each corpus by their OpenAlex `cited_by_count`, with the same tie rule.

<!-- src: scripts/06_citation_bridge.py:top_n (127-134), ref_counts (137-141), build_canons (144-159), sens_grid (442-460); RESULTS_citation_bridge.md:14, 16 -->

**Shared canon, two reported variants.** Some works appear in both top N lists. Two ways of handling them are computed and reported, and the paper will need to state which one carries the main argument [owner decision]. In the shared works removed variant, every work in both top N lists is deleted from both canons, so Canon_P and Canon_L do not overlap, and a bridge paper must cite a work specific to each list. In the shared works kept variant, both full top N lists are used as they are, so a shared work counts toward both canons, and one citation to a shared work can satisfy both sides. The shared works removed variant is computed for both canon definitions (most referenced works and Variant 2), for N = 25, 50 and 100, with merged and unmerged records. The shared works kept variant is computed for the most referenced works definition, for the same N values and both record treatments, and has its own random reference comparison. The trend, year and top paper analyses below were run in the script for the shared works removed variant at N = 50 only, and the tie analysis for the shared works removed variant at N = 25, 50 and 100. Running them for the shared works kept variant would need a change to the script.

<!-- src: scripts/06_citation_bridge.py:152-156 (eff_p, eff_l), 444-460 (variants), 587-597 (kept null), 565-612, 684-727; RESULTS_citation_bridge.md:15, 80-117, 151-160, 123 -->

**Bridge paper and threshold.** A bridge paper is a corpus paper whose merged reference list contains at least one work in Canon_P and at least one work in Canon_L. A stricter version requires at least two works in each canon. The denominator is the set of papers with a nonempty reference list, and never all papers. Corpus membership of each bridge paper is recorded as polycrisis, LtG or both.

<!-- src: scripts/06_citation_bridge.py:find_bridges (162-169), 531-536, 673-682; RESULTS_citation_bridge.md:17, 18 -->

**Random reference comparison (two null models).** Both models keep each tested paper's own number of references, draw that many distinct works at random, and count bridge papers against the real canons. In the within corpus model, a polycrisis only paper draws from the pooled references of polycrisis papers, an LtG only paper from the pooled references of LtG papers, and a paper in both corpora from the union of the two. Works are drawn with probability proportional to the number of tested papers in that pool that cite them, and a repeat of a work within one paper is redrawn. In the pooled model, every paper draws from the references of all tested papers together. Both models are run on merged records. Each uses 1,000 draws with a fixed seed (Section 2.7), and we report the mean, the 5th and 95th percentiles and the maximum of the bridge counts. The within corpus model is run for the shared works removed variant at N = 25, 50 and 100 with one and two references per side, and again for the shared works kept variant. The pooled model is run for the shared works removed variant only. Neither model includes topic structure inside a field.

<!-- src: scripts/06_citation_bridge.py:null_bridge (357-399), 570-597; RESULTS_citation_bridge.md:127, 140, 151, 162 -->

**Tie handling for the canon.** When more works share the cutoff count than there are open slots, the canon depends on the tie rule. We therefore repeat the bridge count with the canon built three ways: by id text order, by a strict canon that keeps only works above the cutoff count (so it can hold fewer than N works), and by random tie breaking among the tied works (500 draws, mean and 5th to 95th percentile). This is run for N = 25, 50 and 100, with one and two references per side, on merged records and the most referenced works definition only. Within each tie variant, works that fall in both lists are removed from both, as in the shared works removed variant. For Variant 2, the number of papers tied at the cutoff count is reported for each N.

<!-- src: scripts/06_citation_bridge.py:tie_bridge (402-435), 599-612, 880-889; RESULTS_citation_bridge.md:164-177 (tied works and open slots per side are in the table at line 170) -->

**Further sensitivity analyses.** (1) Merged records against unmerged records. (2) The four reference list rules in Section 2.3. (3) Keeping or dropping references that point to the merged paper itself. (4) Restricting the tested papers to those published in or after three cutoff years, taken from the canon works' own publication years: the median year of the pooled canon works rounded up (canon works with no year are left out of the medians and of the newest year; a work's year is looked up in the metadata cache, then in the pool lookup, then in the corpus CSV for corpus papers, and a merged work takes the earliest year among its records), the higher of the two canon medians cut to a whole year (not rounded), and the newest canon work year. A paper cannot cite a work published after it. For a merged cited work the year used is the earliest year among its records, and the type is that of the earliest record. (5) Counts of bridge papers by year from 2000 (the script also prints the share of tested papers per year, and sets a flag that records whether the yearly shares, from the median canon year onward and for years with at least 10 tested papers, never fall from one year to the next, with at least three such years; the note's wording about a rise depends on that flag), and by five periods (2000 to 2014, 2015 to 2019, 2020 to 2022, 2023 to 2024, 2025 to 2026), with years that have fewer than 10 tested papers marked as unstable. Papers without a year are left out of the yearly counts. 2026 is a partial year. (6) The number of bridge papers that are themselves canon works, and how many of them are shared canon works. (7) A list of the bridge papers with the highest `cited_by_count`, using standard competition ranking (equal counts share a rank), showing all papers tied at the last shown rank.

### 2.5 Keyword analysis

**Keyword source.** Terms come from the `keywords` and `concepts` columns of the two paper CSVs, so they are OpenAlex keyword and concept tags and not author keywords. Each cell is split on semicolons, trimmed, lower cased and kept if longer than 2 characters, and a paper counts a term once. The CSVs hold at most the first 10 keywords and the 5 highest scoring concepts per record. In the raw records, 691 of 2,616 papers have more than 10 keywords (the most is 26) and 1,965 have more than 5 concepts, so those papers lose tags. A fixed stopword list of 44 terms is then removed. It contains generic discipline labels (for example `sociology`, `mathematics`, `history`), biology labels linked to growth studies in biology (for example `botany`, `cell biology`, `nutrient`), and a few disambiguation labels, plus `crisis response` and `epistemology`. Any term containing an opening parenthesis is also removed. A paper with no term left is called a paper with no usable term and is left out of every keyword measure. The stopword list is the one in `scripts/05_simple_viz.py`, and the keyword script checks at start that its copy is equal.

<!-- src: scripts/07_temporal_keywords.py:74-101, 106-117; scripts/05_simple_viz.py:33-49, 56-63; scripts/02_bibliometric_analysis.py:107-120; RESULTS_temporal_keywords.md:11, 97 -->

**Comparison with the earlier keyword route.** An earlier analysis made a top 50 comparison from term frequency files saved by `scripts/02_bibliometric_analysis.py` (the 100 most frequent terms per corpus, counted over all CSV rows) and read by `scripts/05_simple_viz.py`, which takes the first 50 terms of each cleaned file. The keyword script recomputes the full period top 50 lists directly from the paper CSVs and compares them with that route (term counts, term sets and overlap). The routes differ in three ways. First, the CSVs hold repeated ids (1,500 polycrisis rows for 1,488 ids, 1,154 LtG rows for 1,135), and the earlier route counts each row while the keyword script keeps each id once. Counting every row reproduces the saved counts with no mismatch in the 50 terms of each saved list, and counting each id once changes the count of 12 polycrisis terms and 22 LtG terms in those lists. Second, `02` stores an empty keyword cell as the text `nan`, which `05` drops and the keyword script treats as empty. Third, the tie rule at the cutoff differs: the keyword script breaks ties alphabetically, and the earlier route follows the order of the saved file. The Results note reports the comparison.

<!-- src: scripts/02_bibliometric_analysis.py:95-149; scripts/05_simple_viz.py:117-118, 147-153; scripts/07_temporal_keywords.py:291-334; RESULTS_temporal_keywords.md:12 -->

**Co occurrence files and network figures.** `scripts/02_bibliometric_analysis.py` (its working copy has uncommitted changes that matter: the committed version reads the broad LtG CSV `ltg_papers.csv`, the working copy prefers the refined CSV, so running the committed version gives different frequency and pair files) builds, for each corpus, the same keyword and concept term sets per paper (lower cased, longer than 2 characters, repeated ids not removed, and without the stopword list). It builds each paper's terms as a Python set and counts them in set iteration order, so the order of tied terms in the saved frequency file depends on the Python hash seed (`PYTHONHASHSEED`) and can change between runs, as can the first 50 terms read by `05` and `04` when counts tie at the cutoff. The pair files do not depend on the hash seed (identical under hash seeds 1, 2 and 3 in a check that is not recorded in a script). It counts pairs of terms that occur in the same paper, keeps terms that occur in at least 3 papers and pairs that occur in at least 2 papers, and saves the pair counts and the 100 most frequent terms. It also prints, from the CSV name strings, the 15 most frequent author names and institutions and the 20 most cited papers per corpus. `scripts/04_network_viz.py` draws keyword networks from the pair files (terms with pair weight of at least 5, the 60 terms with the highest total weight), after removing its own list of 12 generic terms, which is not the list of Section 2.5. It draws co authorship networks from the author name strings in the CSV `authors` column (at most the first five authors of each paper, and for a paper with more than five authors the text "et al." sits on the fifth name, which is dropped, so at most four names are used: 123 of 1,500 polycrisis rows and 24 of 1,154 refined LtG rows carry that suffix, counted for this draft from the CSV `authors` column; `02`'s author list drops it in the same way; names are matched as text, not as OpenAlex author ids; each CSV row is counted, so repeated ids are counted again, as in the pair files of `02`), keeping the 80 most frequent authors with at least 2 papers (ties at the 80 author cutoff are ordered by the iteration order of a Python set, so they are not fixed between runs), and dropping authors with no link to another kept author. It draws one network of the terms shared by the first 40 terms of the two frequency files. The polycrisis co authorship graph reads `polycrisis_papers.csv`, and the LtG co authorship graph reads the refined LtG CSV if present and the broad CSV otherwise. The keyword and convergence graphs read the files written by `02`.

**Periods.** The corpora are split into three publication periods: up to 2022, 2023 to 2024 and 2025 to 2026. The bins were chosen after looking at the counts of papers per year, so that each bin has about 50 or more papers with a usable term in both corpora; the script stops if a bin has fewer than 45. This is a post hoc choice. The polycrisis corpus has few papers before 2023, so all papers up to 2022 form one bin. The LtG corpus has fewer than 50 papers with a usable term in each of 2023 and 2024, so those two years are merged for the main analysis and shown singly for reference only. Papers without a publication year are in no period. A full period row uses every paper with a usable term, with or without a year. The latest date in the data is March 29, 2026, so the last period is not a complete two years.

<!-- src: scripts/07_temporal_keywords.py:56-66 (BINS, REFERENCE_ONLY, MIN_PAPERS), 241-248, 361-374; RESULTS_temporal_keywords.md:13, 16, 26, 100 -->

**Overlap and similarity measures.** For each period and for the full period we compute, from the papers with a usable term: (a) the number of terms shared by the top 50 lists of the two corpora; (b) the Jaccard index of the two top 50 sets (saved in the output CSV); (c) a weighted Jaccard index over all terms, using each term's share of papers in each corpus (sum of the smaller share divided by sum of the larger share); and (d) the cosine similarity of term vectors in which each paper adds a total weight of 1 spread equally over its terms. Measures (c) and (d) do not depend on a top 50 cutoff. For each period we also list the shared terms, and the terms that enter each corpus's top 50 compared with the previous period. A term has entered when it is in this period's strict set and was not among the previous period's candidates (the previous strict set plus every term tied at the previous cutoff). A term is newly shared by the same rule applied to both corpora.

<!-- src: scripts/07_temporal_keywords.py:143-170, 398-437, 440-449; RESULTS_temporal_keywords.md:14, 65 -->

**Tie handling for the top 50.** Many terms can share the count at rank 50, so the top 50 overlap is computed under three tie rules: alphabetical order (the rule used for the saved top keyword lists), a strict set that keeps only terms with a count above the cutoff count, and random tie breaking over 1,000 seeded draws (mean and 5th to 95th percentile). The cutoff count, the number of tied terms and the number of open slots are saved for each period and corpus.

<!-- src: scripts/07_temporal_keywords.py:135-140, 173-188, 198-211, 345-346, 415-416; RESULTS_temporal_keywords.md:14, 37-46 -->

**Per period tag measures and the rule for the note's rise wording.** For each period the script saves the mean number of distinct terms per paper with a usable term and the share of papers in the period with no usable term, for each corpus. It also sets flags that choose the note's sentence about a rise: the sentence says the measures rise only if the tie robust overlap means rise at every step, the 5th to 95th ranges of consecutive periods do not overlap, the cosine similarity rises at every step, and the size matched overlap and size matched cosine means rise at every step with non overlapping ranges. Otherwise the note says the data do not show a steady rise.

<!-- src: scripts/07_temporal_keywords.py:408-410, 491-499, 500-505; RESULTS_temporal_keywords.md:81, 89 -->

**Size matched control.** The corpora differ in size within each period, so each period row is also computed on equal sized random samples. In each of 300 draws, the smaller of 60 and the number of papers with a usable term available is drawn without replacement from each corpus (60 is below the smallest main period corpus of 75), and the top 50 overlap (with random tie breaking), the weighted Jaccard index and the cosine similarity are computed. We report the mean and the 5th to 95th percentile. The control is not a correction of the observed values. Where a draw takes at least half of a corpus, the spread understates sampling uncertainty, and the note lists those cases.

<!-- src: scripts/07_temporal_keywords.py:67-68, 382-393, 402-407; RESULTS_temporal_keywords.md:15, 48-54, 81 -->

**Duplicate record sensitivity.** The main keyword tables remove repeated records by OpenAlex id only. As a sensitivity analysis, the same title and first author rule as the bridge analysis (Section 2.2) is applied inside each corpus. Within a merged group the record kept is the one with the highest `cited_by_count`, then the one with the most distinct terms, then the first OpenAlex id in text order. The overlap measures are recomputed on the merged sets with their own seeded tie draws.
